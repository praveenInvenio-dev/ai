# Educational Video Strategy Research — Increment 49 (25 October 2026)

## Purpose
Research-informed prompt and content-planning improvements for AI Story Studio across Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, YouTube long-form explainers, school tutoring, JEE/NEET preparation, and younger children's educational videos. This is a content strategy, not a promise of reach or a claim that one format is algorithmically preferred.

## Evidence reviewed

1. **YouTube Shorts discovery and search guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en
   - YouTube says Shorts ranking is personalized and does not favor a particular type or format.
   - Signals described include whether viewers choose to view or ignore, average view duration, average percentage viewed, likes and post-watch survey results. Topic interest, competition, seasonality and viewer personalization also affect distribution.
   - Strategy implication: build a clear opening, show the promised value early, keep the explanation moving, and test topics based on audience response. Do not claim a fixed viral formula.

2. **YouTube recommendation performance guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en
   - YouTube groups performance into appeal, engagement and satisfaction; it recommends a clear title/thumbnail promise, concise hooks and analytics-led iteration.
   - Strategy implication: concept generation should package a real learner problem; scripts must fulfill the title's promise, not merely maximize suspense.

3. **YouTube recommendation system overview** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en
   - Recommendations are personalized and use viewer history, topic affinity, performance and satisfaction; YouTube advises quality over quantity and encourages series/playlists that help viewers continue learning.
   - Strategy implication: make self-contained episodes with a logical next lesson and consistent topic series. No forced daily posting cadence.

4. **YouTube Shorts analytics guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en
   - Creator Studio surfaces metrics including shown in feed, chose to view versus swiped away, views, likes and subscribers.
   - Strategy implication: use these metrics to compare hook experiments and topic/format cohorts. Do not judge Shorts on raw views alone.

5. **YouTube audience retention guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en
   - Retention curves can reveal intros that do not match the title/thumbnail promise, top moments, spikes and dips.
   - Strategy implication: move high-value demonstrations earlier when retention shows that viewers prefer them; inspect spikes carefully because a spike can mean either rewatch/share or confusion.

6. **Instagram creator Best Practices hub** — https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/
   - Instagram's Professional Dashboard provides personalized advice covering creation, engagement, reach, monetization and guidelines.
   - Strategy implication: treat the in-product creator guidance and account insights as the operational source of truth; avoid generic advice about an alleged single algorithm.

7. **Instagram ranking signals reporting from Instagram leadership** — https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/
   - Reporting on Adam Mosseri's January 2025 guidance identified watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as important Reels signals, with sends especially relevant for reaching non-followers.
   - Strategy implication: make a useful takeaway that viewers naturally want to send to a friend/classmate. This is a creative goal, not a guarantee or a reason to ask for shares instead of teaching.

8. **YouTube Shorts format limits and analytics distinction** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en
   - Shorts can be up to three minutes; YouTube distinguishes total views from engaged views in its analytics.
   - Strategy implication: select duration based on the learning objective, and compare engaged-view metrics rather than interpreting starts/replays as proof of learning.

9. **Official JEE Main syllabus pages** — https://jeemain.nta.nic.in/document/syllabus-2026/ and https://jeemain.nta.nic.in/document/syllabus-2027/
   - Strategy implication: for exam-focused generation, syllabus year matters. Do not claim syllabus alignment, PYQ status, weightage or exam predictions unless the exact official source/version has been checked.

10. **Official NEET UG 2026 syllabus notice** — https://nta.ac.in/Download/Notice/Notice_20260108180635.pdf
    - The NTA notice says the exam is conducted based on the syllabus finalized and notified by the relevant medical education board.
    - Strategy implication: preserve source/year metadata for NEET learning paths and label unverified questions as original practice rather than PYQs.

11. **YouTube Made for Kids guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en
    - Audience designation is a policy decision; Made for Kids content has restrictions such as comments, notifications, personalized advertising and some other features.
    - Strategy implication: never auto-label content based only on a word such as “kids”. Provide a human review step for audience designation and avoid engagement CTAs that pressure children.

12. **YouTube medical misinformation policy** — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13813322?hl=en
    - Educational/scientific context does not grant permission to promote harmful medical misinformation.
    - Strategy implication: NEET biology can explain anatomy and physiology, but health claims must remain evidence-based and not turn exam content into diagnosis/treatment advice.

13. **YouTube original Shorts guidance** — https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en
    - YouTube says it is prioritizing original content and reducing reach for re-uploaded material without meaningful original value.
    - Strategy implication: generate original worked examples, diagrams, narration, story framing and visual explanations; do not imitate or lightly repackage other creators' work.

## Content strategy by lane

### A. JEE / NEET / board-exam videos
- One learning objective per short; one connected set of objectives per long-form lesson.
- Use an intuition-first visual, state the correct rule/formula, solve a complete representative problem, check units/signs/conditions, expose a common trap, and finish with a retrieval question.
- Label every practice item as one of: verified PYQ (with year/session/shift/source), syllabus-style original question, or illustrative example. Never fabricate PYQ provenance or predicted questions.
- JEE Physics: motion/force diagrams, vectors, axes, correctly scaled graphs, units and derivation steps.
- JEE/NEET Chemistry: balanced equations, particles/bonds, oxidation state/charge where relevant, molecular structure, energy profiles and worked numericals.
- NEET Biology: accurate labelled diagrams, process sequences, comparison tables and misconception checks. Visual simplification must not reverse or invent biological mechanisms.
- Support Hindi, English and Indian regional languages; maintain correct scientific terms and consistent symbols across languages.
- Build playlists/series in prerequisite order, but every short must have its own complete takeaway.

### B. School tutoring / younger learners
- Infer the age range and adapt vocabulary, sentence length, examples, humour and visual density.
- One clear objective, familiar context, visible cause-and-effect, one prediction question, reveal with explanation, and a gentle recap.
- Avoid manipulative urgency, shame, unsafe demonstrations, personal-data requests and pressure to like/comment/subscribe.
- Do not assume all “educational” videos are or are not Made for Kids; audience designation requires human review against platform rules and the intended audience.

### C. YouTube long-form explanation tutorials
- Title and thumbnail should promise one clear outcome or learner question.
- Open by demonstrating the problem/result and explaining why it matters; avoid a long intro/logo.
- Structure chapters as learner questions, not a list of definitions. Include prerequisites when necessary, a coherent running example, complete worked example, limitations, recap and a natural next lesson.
- Use visual variation to aid understanding: rich generated context images, accurate programmatic diagrams, graphs/equations, worked examples, on-screen questions and short recap screens. Motion should explain a state change, not decorate every sentence.
- Make chapters and descriptions accurately reflect the lesson. Never pad length to hit a duration target.

### D. Shorts/Reels discovery
- Hook types to test: misconception check, visible experiment, outcome/problem, exam trap, prediction/reveal, relatable everyday connection, before/after, quick diagnosis.
- Show the first meaningful evidence or worked step quickly. The question must be resolved within the short; no artificial “wait until the end”.
- Use captions and mobile-safe layouts. For equations and graphs, keep one readable focal point and highlight the current step.
- End on a useful takeaway or a question that tests understanding. CTA is optional and comes after value; for child-directed content avoid engagement pressure.
- Create a short that naturally points to a deeper long-form lesson when appropriate, but never make the short incomplete solely to force a click.

## Rich visual production rules
- Image generation (e.g. ComfyUI/Wan) supplies atmosphere, environments, characters and illustrative context. It must not be the authority for spelling, labels, equations, numerical values, graph geometry or anatomical accuracy.
- Use deterministic renderers (Manim, Motion Canvas, SVG/HTML/Canvas overlays, KaTeX and/or the existing scene renderer) for equations, graphs, vectors, labelled structures, code, units, axes and answer reveals.
- Preserve a consistent visual identity across a series, but vary scene composition and teaching method when it helps learning.
- Use image-to-video animation only where movement is meaningful and maintain diagram correctness by composing the exact labels/graphs on top.
- Run a quality pass for legibility, mathematical correctness, narration/visual alignment, pronunciation, caption alignment, contrast and safe margins before publishing.

## Trend awareness without trend hallucination
- Stable demand: foundational concepts, prerequisites, common errors, worked examples and exam practice.
- Seasonal demand: syllabus releases, application dates, exam windows, revision periods and result periods. Confirm dates and notices from official sources before publishing.
- Fast trends: current memes, audio, news or platform trends. These require a fresh source/platform trend check with timestamp. If the pipeline has not checked a current source, the script must not say “trending now”.
- Rank candidate topics using a transparent editorial score (not an algorithm prediction): audience relevance, learning value, visual demonstrability, novelty of angle, source confidence, production feasibility and series fit. Apply accuracy/safety as gates, not scores.
- Test two or three genuinely different hooks on the same learning objective, then compare the platform metrics for similar audience cohorts. Avoid changing topic, hook, length, voice and visual style all at once, which makes results hard to interpret.

## Increment 51 implementation note — programmatic-only Concept Explainer
Concept Explainer videos do not use Wan, ComfyUI-generated still images, or image-to-video. Their visuals are authored by the existing Motion Canvas, Three.js, Manim, and native diagram renderers. Wan remains available only to separate story/cartoon flows. If a programmatic renderer is unavailable, the existing native diagram fallback should be used.
